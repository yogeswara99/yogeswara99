# Impact Statement Rebuilder
### Covers: The Impact Statement Rebuilder

Before/after transformations applied across the three resumes. Where your original source material already had a real metric, it's preserved (per your confirmation these are accurate) — only the framing changes. Where a figure would look thin at Director/GM altitude, it's re-expressed around trajectory and percentage-of-target rather than the raw absolute number, per your instruction.

---

**Before (base CV, responsibility-framed):**
> "Increasing the overall sales performance by analyzing, evaluating and implementing the most effective sales strategies on monthly and yearly basis to ensure all goals and KPI targets are achieved."

**After (ownership + outcome-framed):**
> "Own regional sales strategy across 8 MENA markets, directing channel segmentation and performance-based partner programs — delivering 15–20% year-on-year channel revenue growth."

*Why it's stronger:* the "before" describes an activity with no result attached; the "after" states the scope owned and the measurable outcome in one breath.

---

**Before:**
> "Track record of success in launching the TBS product vertical by OBO Bettermann in the market, leading to a substantial and sustained increase in sales over a span of three years."

**After:**
> "Built a new product vertical from zero market presence to an established, sustainably growing revenue line within three years — reaching ~70% of annual sales target by Year 3 — appointing and developing 7+ new distribution partners across the region in the process."

*Why it's stronger:* "substantial and sustained increase" is a vague self-assessment with nothing to verify; "zero to ~70% of target in three years, 7+ partners appointed" is specific and interview-defensible. Note this deliberately drops the AED 2–5M absolute figure from the Eaton/Siemens drafts — at a Director-level altitude that number reads as small; the percentage-of-target and zero-to-established trajectory carry the same true story without inviting an unfavorable size comparison.

---

**Before:**
> "Maintaining regular communication and providing continuous support to regional consultants and clients, resulting in OBO Bettermann consistently being included in their list of preferred vendors."

**After:**
> "Sustain preferred-vendor status with regional consultants and EPC contractors through structured technical and commercial engagement — directly supporting specification wins on competitive projects."

*Why it's stronger:* "maintaining regular communication" is an input, not an outcome; "sustain preferred-vendor status... supporting specification wins" states the commercial consequence.

---

**Before:**
> "Managing, developing, coaching, and training the sales force to improve their skills and ensure that a high professional standard is achieved and monthly sales target along with their KPI goals are met."

**After:**
> "Coach and develop a distributor-facing sales capability through structured training programs spanning technical product knowledge, consultative selling, and CRM discipline — sustaining partner performance against monthly and annual KPI targets."

*Why it's stronger:* four gerunds strung together ("Managing, developing, coaching, and training") reads as an unfiltered task list; the rewrite names the *mechanism* (structured training programs, three specific content areas) and the *result it protects* (KPI attainment).

---

**Before (Siemens Energy draft, overclaimed positioning):**
> "Certified in Green Hydrogen, Green Ammonia, and Renewable Energy — uniquely positioned to support Siemens Energy's Transformation of Industry division in driving decarbonization conversations and green H2 adoption with industrial clients across the MENA region."

**After (defensible, still ambitious):**
> "Certified in Green Hydrogen, Green Ammonia, and Renewable Energy, building on an engineering and industrial channel-sales foundation to bring commercial and technical credibility to energy-transition client conversations across the region."

*Why it's stronger:* the original claims a specific, unearned fit to one named division of one named employer — a line that only works if you're actually quoting from their own JD, and otherwise reads as presumptuous to anyone who's screened candidates before. The rewrite states the real asset (certification + engineering/channel background) and lets the reader draw the "good fit" conclusion themselves, which is always more persuasive than asserting it.
